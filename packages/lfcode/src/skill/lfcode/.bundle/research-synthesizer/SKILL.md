# Research Synthesizer

Turn multiple pieces of evidence into a traceable, uncertainty-aware synthesis.

## Workflow

1. Clarify the question, audience, time boundary, geographic or technical scope, desired depth, and acceptable source types.
2. Form search or inspection questions before collecting evidence. Prefer primary or authoritative sources, then use secondary sources to cross-check context.
3. Record source title, publisher or owner, URL or local path, publication or access date, relevant passage, and scope for every material claim.
4. Compare sources for agreement, definitions, incentives, recency, missing data, and contradictions. Separate direct observations, calculations, interpretations, and recommendations.
5. Draft a concise synthesis with citations or evidence locations, confidence and caveats. Never fill a gap with a plausible but unsupported statement.
6. Recheck every important number, quote, date, and causal claim against its source before delivery.

## Boundaries

- Do not imply live or exhaustive research when network access, source coverage, or freshness is limited.
- Do not fabricate citations, quotes, browsing results, or source metadata.
- Do not collect sensitive personal information or access restricted sources without explicit authorization.

## Completion check

Label facts, inferences, and unresolved questions clearly. Include the evidence trail and the cutoff time used for the synthesis.
